03 / ARCHITECTURE SUMMARY

Connection path and analysis roles

Based on article Figures 1–5 and their accompanying workflow descriptions. These are conceptual roles, not a claim that every tool ran in one serial chain.

  1. Legitimate Wi-Fi + rogue AP

    The testbed compares a legitimate connection with an Evil Twin that imitates the network identity. Deauthentication and signal attractiveness are part of the reported connection tests.

  2. Researcher-owned test device

    The device connects to the rogue AP; Wifiphisher presents a simulated captive portal in the relevant experiment.

  3. Researcher-controlled analysis environment

    Traffic passes through the test environment toward destination services. Wireshark observes packets. Burp Suite and mitmproxy perform the respective proxy experiments.

  4. Protocol and control evaluation

    Compare HTTP/DNS visibility with TLS metadata, HSTS downgrade resistance, certificate rejection, and pinning behavior.

Defensive takeaways

  • The study links risk to network selection, deceptive captive portals, and user decisions about submitting sensitive information.
  • Its conclusions favor secure communications, appropriate wireless security settings, and rogue-AP monitoring.
  • The documented HSTS, pinning, and certificate-rejection outcomes show why joining an untrusted network and exposing application contents are separate questions.
